Lesson 3: Defining Brand Voice, Audience & Buyer Personas

1. Introduction

Two brands can sell the exact same product and sound completely different online — one playful and irreverent, one polished and authoritative. Neither is wrong. What matters is that the voice matches the audience, and that every post reinforces a consistent identity rather than feeling random from day to day.

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:


Define brand voice and explain why consistency matters.
Identify the key research methods used to understand a target audience.
Build a basic buyer persona to guide content decisions.


2. Core Concepts & Explanations

What is Brand Voice?

Brand voice is the consistent personality and tone a brand uses across all its communication — the words it chooses, the humor (or lack of it), the level of formality, and how it responds to its audience. It's the difference between a brand that sounds like a helpful friend and one that sounds like a corporate press release.

Common brand voice archetypes include: playful, authoritative, empathetic, bold, minimalist, and conversational. Most brands land somewhere between two of these rather than at a single extreme.

Researching Your Audience

Before you can define a voice or plan content, you need real information about who you're talking to — not assumptions. Common research methods include:


Social media insights/analytics — platform-native data on who already follows and engages with the account
Surveys and direct customer conversations — asking existing customers directly about their preferences and pain points
Competitor analysis — observing who engages with similar brands and what content resonates
Comments and DMs — the language and questions your audience already uses


Building a Buyer Persona

A buyer persona is a semi-fictional profile representing your ideal audience member, built from real research rather than guesswork. A basic persona includes:

A well-built persona keeps content decisions grounded — instead of "what should we post today," the question becomes "what would Ada actually want to see today."

3. Practical Application / How-To

Using your Lesson 1-2 business, build a one-page buyer persona: give them a name, a goal, a pain point, and note which platform(s) they're most likely to be active on (referencing what you decided in Lesson 2).
